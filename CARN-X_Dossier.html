Document 1 / Minimum Viable Product

The smallest thing that delivers an honest probability

A local console that trains one probabilistic model on real MSTR history and serves its calibrated distribution, a fair Monte-Carlo cone, and a classical-statistics second opinion — with the out-of-sample evidence attached.

The core loop

Four steps. Steps 1–3 run once (or on demand); step 4 is every screen, instant, from a cached checkpoint.

  1. Fetch — daily OHLCV for MSTR plus 11 context assets from Yahoo Finance, split- and dividend-adjusted, aligned to the NYSE calendar, cached to parquet.
  2. Feature & target — ~265 strictly-causal features; vol-normalized, de-trended forward-log-return targets at horizons 1 / 5 / 20 days.
  3. Train once — a 9-seed ensemble of the hybrid net on all history (~11 min, CPU); persisted with a schema fingerprint.
  4. Serve — nine screens load the checkpoint and render the predictive distribution, the Monte-Carlo cone, the probability lab, the BTC-cycle scenario, and the walk-forward evidence.

Scope — in and out for v1

In scope shipped

Full Student-t predictive distribution (μ, σ, ν) per horizon · calibrated direction probability · Monte-Carlo price cone with four drift modes · first-passage / terminal / multi-touch probabilities · VaR & CVaR at 1% and 5% · 25+ classical distributions with E / Var / SD · GARCH(1,1) & 2-state Markov · BTC halving-cycle structural scenario · walk-forward OOS evidence with calibration diagnostics · advisory position sizing · one-click retrain.

Out of scope deferred

Intraday horizons · options greeks / implied-vol surface · live order routing or brokerage integration · multi-ticker portfolios · the L5 macro-micro fusion layer (present, off by default) · Engine A (structural bivariate Merton-jump BTC + Ornstein-Uhlenbeck mNAV Monte-Carlo) · Sobol quasi-random sequences · hosted / multi-user deployment.

Definition of done

  • test_app.py — 10 steps, green (data, inference, every screen, the real train button, integrity, orchestrator conformance, MC engine).
  • stress_test.py — 7 sections, green (chart fuzz, MC invariant sweep, calendar round-trip, config, model staleness, prob-model extremes, 174-mutation widget sweep).
  • test_integrity.py and test_prob_math.py — green.
  • A production_xl checkpoint exists; every non-Settings screen loads it in under a second.
  • The walk-forward report is reproducible and its calibration numbers are documented (see §3.8).
Document 2 / Market Requirements

Why this tool has to exist

MSTR is one of the most-analysed and worst-served names for a retail analyst who wants a probability rather than a headline. Three failure modes dominate the alternatives.

The problem, in three framings

Failure modeWhat it looks likeWhy it misleads
Point-target theatre "MSTR price target: $432." No probability is attached. A number with no distribution around it can't be sized, hedged, or falsified.
Naive-GBM artifact A generic Monte-Carlo calculator returns "93% chance MSTR is lower in 60 days." Negative backward-looking drift plus the −½σ² volatility drag at ~85% annualized vol mechanically forces most paths down. A near-certain decline only exists near insolvency — not for a going concern.
Assumption laundered as forecast "If BTC reaches $150k then MSTR is $432" — presented as a prediction. The conditional is an assumption the reader supplied. The probability of the antecedent is never stated, so the whole chain inherits false confidence.

Who it's for

  • Primary — a single quant-curious MSTR holder or independent analyst who wants institutional methodology (calibrated distributions, walk-forward validation, fat-tailed innovations) running on a laptop, not a Bloomberg terminal.
  • Secondary — anyone stress-testing an MSTR thesis who needs to see the same question from the neural, the classical-statistics, and the BTC-cycle angle at once.

Market context that shapes the requirements

  • MSTR trades as a leveraged Bitcoin-treasury proxy. Its multi-month path is BTC-driven; its rolling beta to BTC is ~1.4–1.5.
  • BTC's multi-year path has historically organised around the ~4-year halving cycle, with collapsing amplitude each cycle (2016 ≈ ×100, 2020 ≈ ×7, 2024 ≈ ×1.8).
  • The stock carries an mNAV premium over its Bitcoin holdings and a Saylor reflexivity flywheel (issue shares above NAV → buy BTC → raise sats-per-share).
  • Post the 2024 10:1 split and in the ETF era, realized annualized volatility sits at 82–88%. As of the current panel: last close $127.31, all-time high $473.83 (2024-11-20), −73% drawdown.

Alternatives and why they fall short

AlternativeGap
Sell-side analyst price targetsScenario, not probability. No distribution, no calibration record.
TradingView / charting indicatorsNo forward distribution; no fat tails; no walk-forward evidence.
Generic Monte-Carlo calculatorsNaive GBM, Gaussian steps, no trading calendar, the drift artifact above.
Bloomberg / institutional platformsInaccessible to the target user, and still point-estimate-centric for a name like this.

Requirements the market imposes

IDRequirementStatus
MRD-01Every forward statement carries a probability or an explicit interval — never a bare point.shipped
MRD-02The tool never claims directional skill it cannot demonstrate out-of-sample.shipped
MRD-03The UI structurally separates an assumption ("if BTC → X") from a model probability ("P(BTC reaches X)").shipped
MRD-04Spot price is the latest split- and dividend-adjusted close; the 10:1 split must not appear as a 10× jump.shipped

MRD-06Horizons are counted in NYSE trading days with US market holidays excluded.shipped

Success criteria

  • Out-of-sample PIT–KS < 0.06 and 90% interval coverage within ±0.04 of the nominal at every horizon.
  • A user can go from a clean checkout to a calibrated distribution with one Train model now click.
  • No screen can present a scenario as if it were a probability.

3.1   System overview — three engines in parallel

Three independent forecasting engines run side by side. All three draw from the same historical panel; none feeds another. They "cross-reference" only in the UI, so the user sees one question from three angles. The neural engine is the flagship; the other two are baselines and scenarios.

3.2   The CARN-X 8-layer architecture

The named orchestrator is carnx.CARNX — one method per layer. config.CarnxConfig is threaded through all of them; python carnx.py prints the live conformance report.

3.7   Configuration system

CarnxConfig is a single object threaded through every layer. It aggregates eight sub-configs (DataConfig, FeatureConfig, TargetConfig, ModelConfig, WFConfig, BaselineConfig, EconConfig, InferenceConfig) plus a Constants singleton (252, 504, RiskMetrics λ = 0.94, z-values, MC path count, 1400-day cycle length) and toggles.

.sync() is the hierarchy that prevents contradiction: it pushes shared fields down so walk_forward.model is inference.model is config.model — the same object. Change d_model once and the whole pipeline follows. It is verified idempotent, and save / load to JSON round-trips.

3.8   Training methodology & the honesty stance

The recipe (per net)

AdamW (lr 1.0–1.2e-3, weight-decay 1.5–2e-4) · CosineAnnealingLR · gradient clip 1.0 · early stopping on a held-out validation tail (patience 8–14) with best-state restore. Per fold, the FoldScaler is fitted on the training slice only and the experts are routed from the training-slice close window.

Ensemble aggregation. μ = mean of members; σ = √(mean(σ²) + var(μ)) — a proper mixture variance that folds in between-model uncertainty; ν and p_up are member means. Then descale: μraw = μscaled·σewma·√h + drifth — the drift is added back (omitting it once drove directional accuracy below 50%; that bug is fixed and guarded).

Does the training actually matter?

Yes for what it is designed to do; no for what it does not claim. Measured out-of-sample across 49 folds, 3,051 rows, 2022-07 → 2026-07:

Plain-language verdict

The 9-seed ensemble genuinely learns the shape, width, tails and regime of the return distribution, and that calibration holds on data it never saw — measurable, not asserted, and not over-fit (the walk-forward harness proves it). It does not learn to call direction: daily direction is a coin flip for every method, and only the 20-day horizon marginally beats chance. The point forecast is worse than "tomorrow = today" in squared error because the short-horizon drift is bearish — which is exactly why the configurable drift modes exist. This is a distribution / volatility / regime instrument, not a direction predictor, and every screen says so.

3.9   Known limitations & non-goals

  • Direction is not predicted. Non-goal by design. Any edge is small, horizon-dependent, and disclosed.
  • The BTC cycle rests on 2–3 prior cycles. The current cycle runs at ~30% of the historical amplitude; the 4-year theory may weaken in the ETF era. The width is the message, not the median.
  • The US-market-holiday list runs through 2028. Beyond that the trading-day count is approximate (weekends only); the app caps the probability-lab horizon at 3 calendar years to stay inside it.
  • L5 macro-micro fusion is off by default. It needs a manually-supplied NAV-premium CSV.
  • The "model" Monte-Carlo drift mode extrapolates a one-day view over a year. Over 252 steps this compounds to an absurd annualized figure — labelled as such; risk-neutral is the default.
  • Single-user, local. No hosting, no auth, no multi-ticker.

3.10   Validation strategy

Four test suites

  • test_integrity.py — the anti-look-ahead guard rails (below).
  • test_prob_math.py — every E / Var / SD and probability formula checked against scipy moments and closed forms.
  • test_app.py — 10 steps: data, tiny fit → save → load → predict → Monte-Carlo, prob-models, an AppTest render of every screen with widget interaction, the real train button, the integrity runner, walk-forward smoke, orchestrator conformance, the Monte-Carlo engine (drift modes + martingale + calendar).
  • stress_test.py — 7 adversarial sections: 50-case chart fuzz, a 196-config Monte-Carlo invariant sweep, a 1,586-case calendar round-trip, config presets, model staleness, prob-model sensor extremes, and a 174-mutation widget sweep that pushes every control on every screen to its edges.

The five anti-look-ahead guarantees

  1. Feature causality — a feature on a truncated panel is byte-identical to the full run sliced to the same date.
  2. No single-feature leak — |corr(featuret, next return)| < 0.5 for every column.
  3. Fold scaling — winsor + standardize statistics come only from the training slice.
  4. Embargo — max(horizon) = 20 rows are skipped between train and test; a training label can never overlap the test window.
  5. Exact target inverse — descale() reproduces the raw forward return, drift included.
